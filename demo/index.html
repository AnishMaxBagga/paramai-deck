The bottleneck

Quantitative Systems Pharmacology engines can predict efficacy and toxicity before a single patient is dosed — if they are fed correct parameters.

Each simulation needs hundreds of kinetic constants: receptor binding rates, hepatic extraction ratios, partition coefficients, intrinsic clearances.

That data is trapped in millions of unstructured papers. PhDs extract it by hand over months. Most programs skip it entirely — and revert to empirical dosing.

That is why drug development still looks more like guessing than science.

what is AUC, and why does it matter?

AUC (area-under-the-curve) is total drug exposure — literally the area beneath the plasma concentration curve over time. It's the single number regulators and clinicians use to decide if a dose is safe and effective: too little → no efficacy, too much → toxicity. AUC0–24 means exposure across the first 24 hours.

At the same 250 mg dose, the naive parameter set produces a curve 27% lower than reality. The clinician concludes the drug is being cleared too fast → recommends a higher dose to compensate → patient actually overdoses, because real clearance is much slower than the naive parameters claimed.
